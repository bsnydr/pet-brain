## Who she is
- Female **Border Collie**, born 2025-11-05 (~7 months). Not spayed.
- Lives in a flat with a small yard; two owners share the daytime handling.
- ~14 kg; food: adult kibble, 2 meals/day. Very toy-motivated (more than food).
- Force-free training only — no punishment or aversive tools.

## ⭐ The core insight for her
Scout is an over-thinker who **winds herself up around motion** (bikes, joggers, other dogs) and then
can't settle. Her "bad" behaviours (barking at the window, nipping heels) are almost always
**over-arousal + under-sleep**, not disobedience. The lever is *less stimulation + more rest*, not more
exercise — a tired collie gets more wired, not calmer.

## Her signals & patterns (owner-observed)
- **Pre-zoomies tell:** she gets "the eyes" — a hard stare and a low crouch — right before a burst.
  Redirect to a chew *then*, before it starts.
- **Needs-the-toilet tell:** sudden sniffing and a tight circle by the back door. She does NOT ask
  otherwise — take her out on that, and always right after waking and eating.
- **Over-threshold tell:** she stops taking treats and fixates. If she refuses a treat outside, she's
  too close to whatever it is — add distance, don't train.
- **Window-barking** spikes when she's under-slept; a covered crate + white noise cuts it right down.

## What works / what doesn't
- **Works:** enforced naps (she needs ~16–18 h/day at this age), sniffy decompression walks, a snuffle
  mat before guests arrive, a "find it" scatter to reset her after a trigger.
- **Doesn't:** long fetch marathons to "tire her out" (revs her up), or asking for focus when she's
  already fixated on a dog.

## Per-trigger handling
- **Other dogs:** distance + treats before she locks on; goal is *neutral*, not greeting. No on-lead
  greetings while she's still reactive.
- **Bikes/joggers:** mark-and-feed the instant she notices, before she lunges; cross the street early.
- **Guests:** crate with a chew until the initial excitement passes, then greet calmly on lead.

## Health flags to raise proactively
- **Border Collies can carry the MDR1 gene** (drug sensitivity). She's **not tested** — flag it before
  any systemic medication or sedation. A cheek-swab test is cheap; it's on the vet to-do.
- Vaccines up to date; next booster and the annual are in the vet log. Flea/tick cover is current.
- ⚠️ Vet (not chat) for: limping, blood in urine/stool, repeated vomiting, a suspected seizure,
  anything medicinal, or eating a toxic food.

## Toxic foods — never feed (vet immediately if eaten)
Xylitol (sugar-free gum/peanut butter) · chocolate · grapes/raisins · onion/garlic · macadamia ·
alcohol & raw dough · cooked bones · caffeine. When in doubt, call the vet or a 24 h emergency clinic.
